## Example acceptance checks

1. Name the essential decision and the plausible wrong method. Choose values for which their outcomes differ. For example, exponent 2 paired with 2 fails to distinguish adding from multiplying; use 3 and 4 first.
2. Use simple but distinct numerical roles. Repeated values, cancellation to one, zero intercepts, equal sides and special angles are deliberate later cases, not substitutes for a general first example.
3. Change one essential feature in a contrast pair; then vary irrelevant features in further positive cases. Follow with mixed recognition so position cannot reveal the method.
4. Require reasons at the decision points. Do not add a verbal explanation to every routine arithmetic line or turn every prerequisite into a public skill.
5. State domain and feasibility conditions. A zero-power statement needs a nonzero base; a denominator must be nonzero; triangle data must be mutually consistent.
6. Preserve exact arithmetic until the requested rounding step. Two inconsistent constructions can round to the same answer: rounded agreement is insufficient evidence of consistency.
7. Mark untaught demands as gaps or explicit extensions. Difficulty labels in the source do not determine which prerequisites have been taught.
