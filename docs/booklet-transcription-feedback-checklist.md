This checklist records the user's general feedback from the Index Laws transcription session on 9 September 2026. It is required for every future transcription run, alongside the [direct compact workflow](booklet-direct-compact-import.md). Apply it to every occurrence in every book, including existing books, unless the user explicitly limits the change. A named example is evidence of a pattern, not a scope limit.

## Source fidelity requirements

For missing numerators and denominators, apply the 14 October 2026
[fraction-component exception](booklet-cross-session-rules.md#cloze-styles-and-source-tables-4-october-2026):
use outlined native maths boxes with sufficient handwriting capacity. A full
fraction scaffold retains its boxed numerator, boxed denominator and vinculum,
even when both components are missing (17 October 2026, user reaffirmed).
Ordinary unstructured answer spaces outside a fraction use dotted clozes.

For ordinary feedback maintenance, use the [minimum verification matrix](booklet-change-runbook.md#minimum-verification-for-feedback-maintenance) and apply only relevant categories below to the affected occurrences. Do not rerun a whole transcription checklist, every historical interaction case or all five complete editions for an ordinary repair. Shared-cause audits and explicitly local exceptions still determine content scope; a narrow verification method must not leave known applicable defects unfixed. The complete transcription/full-acceptance checks later in this document remain for new imports or required full reviews.

1. **Alignment in every teaching box:** preserve source paragraph, label, equation and answer alignment. Centred labels remain centred. Check first expressions, first-line equalities and continuation equals-sign columns separately; do not simply left-align every line or centre every equation block. Related prompt/response equations use explicit `equationAlignment` on the example: `relation` aligns their relation signs and may centre the complete pair; `continuation` reserves the equals-sign gutter on the initial expression so it aligns with the right-hand working below. Keep prompt and solution independently editable. Audit all current books against source; preserve independent equations and explicit local exceptions. Verify fraction heights, equal-sign positions and box containment in screen and PDF. First-baseline question labels apply to text prompts; labels beside diagrams, tables and structured layouts remain at the top. Check both cases after shared alignment changes.
2. **Speech-bubble spacing:** match the source's spacing above and below the character/bubble row and between the surrounding question paragraphs. Use the shared editable bubble template. Character portraits use a cropped **17 mm width**, proportional height, a fixed character track and a **5 mm gap** to the bubble. Keep originals unchanged and store an editable crop. A statement-area width excludes the portrait and gap (108 mm statement + 17 mm portrait + 5 mm gap = 130 mm row). Check all occurrences, including bank copies; surrounding columns must not shrink the portrait. Do not inherit large generic diagram or paragraph gaps.
3. **Space before box text:** remove unintended blank paragraphs and stacked template padding, table margins and cell padding. Preserve deliberate source spacing; do not remove all padding indiscriminately.
4. **Source colours:** Ordinary general-diagram lines and labels use black `#000000`; preserve semantic colours and meaningful shading, with graphs following their separate rules. See [the diagram colour contract](booklet-diagram-colours.md). This overrides decorative diagram source palettes. Use the shared standard palette for every editable text run, equation, annotation, fill and background. Map Index Laws source red `#AA0505` to standard red `#ef6068`, preserving its meaning and original evidence. Custom shades fail acceptance. Editor warning styles must not leak into printed content.
5. **Question grids:** preserve meaningful source column counts, row grouping and reading order, including nested subparts. Inventory the source geometry before laying out content; never certify an imported column count by copying it into the review record. Do not default all questions to two columns. Check the resulting pagination; Index Laws Mixed Practice Q5 requires three columns, Exercise 8 Q7b is three columns by two rows, and Exercise 10 Q3 is four columns. Four parts default to a 2 by 2 grid (10 October 2026, user requested); use another count only for a recorded spacing reason. A short last row keeps the grid's columns, so each part sits under the part above it; the renderer does this for generated rows and leaves rows that were resized or regrouped by hand.
6. **Matching cards:** use editable cards with source-supported widths, spacing, placement and external labels. Avoid stretching small cards across the available page width.
7. **Consistent mathematical typography:** ordinary equations, worked steps and mathematical scaffolds use native editable maths, not screenshots with different fonts or sizing. Power-of-product/quotient arrows use anchored native annotations. Retain original rasters as evidence. A necessary retained raster needs an explicit reason and a final-size visual check.
   Presentation verifier v3 requires an occurrence-specific raster exception; images inside rich-text documents count too. Review the complete scaffold, not just its first expression. For consecutive fraction steps, allow readable vertical separation (the shared equation renderer uses at least 8 pt row spacing across all books and answer editions), preserving larger physical writing-space gaps, colours and equals-sign alignment.
8. **Completion blanks and tables:** follow the [4 October cloze/table convention](booklet-cross-session-rules.md#cloze-styles-and-source-tables-4-october-2026). Use editable native dotted clozes for ordinary completion blanks, including boxed source blanks, with sufficient handwriting capacity in exponents. Missing fraction numerators and denominators use outlined native maths boxes, including teaching and in-place answer scaffolds; do not recommend dots for these components. Retain other boxes only for a specific purpose or an explicit local exception. Preserve repeated-factor expansions, intermediate powers and final-answer steps; reflow before reducing writing capacity. Keep source tables as native tables, preserving meaningful rows, columns and borders. Bordered response cells normally have no dotted leaders or duplicate boxes. Actual card activities remain cards.
9. **Numbered Key Ideas:** keep each number beside the first line of its text, with a consistent label gutter and hanging continuation text. Verify both editable and print views. Inline cloze blanks must not acquire additional working space.
10. **Image alignment controls:** the focused image menu must expose Left/Centre/Right visibly. Block and inline images must also have a usable alignment route. Preserve placement through applying edits, resizing, saving and reopening; never silently reset it to left.
11. **Repeated occurrences and future runs:** a reported problem triggers a whole-booklet search for the same pattern and a shared template/renderer correction when applicable. A local content patch alone does not close a recurring renderer defect.
12. **Syllabus outcomes:** transcribe every source syllabus code together with its complete outcome statement and content bullets. Inventory these separately from the cover and topic titles. The cover's omission of syllabus-summary lines does not authorise dropping the Syllabus Content page's outcomes. Compare the exact code against original evidence; do not infer a replacement from topic names.

13. **Redundant measurements:** retain source-given numerical measurements even when they are not needed for the solution. Identifying which givens are relevant is an assessed skill. Unused is not a defect. Check consistency with the diagram and its stated rounding precision before proposing any correction. Do not delete redundant measurements or replace them with symbolic labels merely to simplify a diagram. Correct confirmed numerical inconsistencies with recorded replacement values while preserving the selection task; keep uncertain repairs open for review.

14. **Exercise headings:** in every questions edition, show each numbered exercise heading once at its first practice section, including starts partway down a teaching page. Source-page sections, teaching checkpoints and pagination continuations must not repeat it. Check the complete rendered booklet, not just the first page of each exercise. Preserve continuous numbering, source sections and pagination settings. Compact answer columns retain exercise context headings.

15. **Question part indents:** every labelled ancestor supplies a 7 mm indent, including custom arrangements and full-width follow-up parts. Check label positions independently of diagram/column placement. Shared rendering restores missing semantic gutters without double-indenting existing nested layouts; see [question part indentation](booklet-question-part-indents.md).

16. **Diagram and working relationships:** apply the [Volume prevention rules](booklet-volume-feedback-2026-09-12.md#prevention-in-future-work). Record source-relative left/right placement of diagrams, calculations and photos, paired-figure rows, and scaffold baseline alignment before layout. Compare isolated cross-sections with the actual cut and verify curved/composite solid silhouettes independently. Use shared annotation and vertical-alignment controls; inspect every complete label and writing blank at final size. Source appearance does not override dotted-scaffold or section-header house style.

17. **Opening syllabus:** combine all syllabus outcomes, focus statements and bullets into one editable rich-text block at the start of the booklet. Use the section title for the shared header. Preserve every source block and any repeated source furniture in provenance, and update inventory mappings for content moved into the combined block. Check the opening page and its teaching neighbour in affected editions.

18. **Multiple-choice options:** option letters are uppercase and bold in every arrangement, including table cells and parallel inline layouts. Keep the option wording regular unless the source gives it independent emphasis. Audit repeated choices across current books and future imports; inspect final-size output.

## Part labels run in one sequence (11 October 2026, user requested)

**Start at a (14 October 2026, user requested).** Every independent alphabetically labelled practice question starts at `a`, even when the source starts later. Retain later letters only when there is a substantive reason: the block continues the same question, or earlier labelled parts are visible completed demonstrations within that question. Source lettering alone is insufficient. Preserve original source labels and stable node IDs, and keep question and answer labels consistent.

Label the parts of one practice question in a single sequence, whatever the source does. When a question has several runs of parts, each under its own instruction or beside its own table or dataset, the second run continues where the first stopped (`a`–`d`, then `e`–`h`), and a lone response between or after the runs takes the next label rather than none. Roman-numeral parts continue the same way (`i`–`iii`, then `iv`–`vi`). Keep each run's instruction and its grouping on the question page; only the labels change. Update any wording that names a part ("use part a").

Restarted labels are not a neutral copy of the source: the answer sections can only tell the runs apart by printing a position number and repeating the run's instruction beside every answer (`20(4)c`). With one sequence the answers read `20a`–`20j`. Record the relabelling as an intentional departure where the source restarts.

`node scripts/booklet/check-part-label-sequence.mjs [project-id]` lists practice questions that restart their labels, with the labels a repair would assign (`--strict` to fail); a new import should report none. It marks for hand repair the cases it cannot sequence safely: questions continued across blocks, more than 26 parts, nested unlabelled groups, pinned source labels and wording that names a part. Teaching blocks are not reported: their responses are filled in place and each example may keep its own `a`, `b`.

## Equation and working alignment (4 October 2026)

The initial import reproduces the source's alignment; it is not left for later editing. For every equation, line of working and worked example, record and encode three things:

1. **Position on the page:** left, centred or right, as the source shows it. Outside a table this is the paragraph's `align`. Inside a table the cell's `align` applies to every line, so set the cell; where lines in one cell differ, set each paragraph's `align` and the cell's `preserveParagraphAlignment`. A paragraph alignment stored in a cell without that flag is ignored at print.
2. **Alignment between lines of working:** relation-aligned steps use one `aligned` environment with `&` at the relation; steps the source centres or left-aligns independently use `gathered` or separate paragraphs. Do not align at the equals sign when the source does not.
3. **Worked-example question against working:** keep the source's column relationship (question beside or above its working, and where the working starts) through the example layout and `example.equationAlignment`.

The independent content review checks these against the source images with the other presentation items. `node scripts/booklet/check-dormant-alignment.mjs <project-id>` lists table lines whose stored alignment their cell overrides; a new import should report none. Existing booklets are reported, not auto-repaired, because either correction changes print. Studio's Layout panel sets all of these directly (Position, Cell alignment with row/column/table scope, and "Only this line").

### Equals-sign gutter (10 October 2026, user requested)

When a left-aligned calculation opens with a bare expression and continues on lines that begin with a relation sign, indent the opening expression by one relation-sign gutter, so it sits directly above the right-hand sides and the relation signs stack in one column:

```
  −5 + 2
= −3
```

This is house style for prompts, teaching examples and short and worked answers in every booklet, whether or not the source shows the offset. Encode it in the way that fits the content:

- **Separate paragraphs:** begin the opening expression with `\mathrel{\phantom{=}}`.
- **One `aligned` equation:** `&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}a+b\\&=c`. Where the next row's relation sits inside a colour group (`&\color{#268cff}{=c}`), wrap the opening row the same way, `&{\mathrel{\phantom{=}}a+b}`, or it prints one thick space too far right.
- **Example prompt with a separate solution:** `equationAlignment: {mode: 'continuation'}`.
- **Cloze scaffolds:** a borderless two-column table with a narrow right-aligned relation column, as in Integers source page 26 question 2.

The opening expression is left-aligned with its working: an expression centred above left-aligned working (as in Operations with Fractions and Decimals source page 27 question 1) moves left and takes the gutter. Use the standard gutter only; do not make one by hand with `\quad`, `\ ` or `\hspace`.

It does not apply when the first line already carries the relation (`a&=b\\&=c` or `&a+b\\={}&c`), where the working itself is centred, or to independent equations that are not steps of one calculation. `node scripts/booklet/check-equals-gutter.mjs [project-id]` lists opening expressions with no gutter (`--list` for every booklet, `--strict` to fail); a new import should report none under "need a gutter". Existing booklets are reported, not auto-repaired: the user decides which to change.
