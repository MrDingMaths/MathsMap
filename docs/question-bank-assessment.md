# Question-bank assessment

The September 2026 Linear Relationships assessment replaces the original builder's category defaults with individual readings of all 180 bank records. Ten source-labelled Review questions remain available to pinned booklets and teaching modules, but have `libraryRole: "theory-review"` and are excluded from the practice manifest, filters and bank assembly candidates. Three existing draft continuation records also remain unlisted, leaving 167 selectable questions. They are not deleted from the accepted booklet. Guided practice and Identify tasks are not excluded merely because they occur near theory.

Scores describe estimated cognitive demand on a prepared student who has been taught the relevant booklet methods. They are not empirical success rates or measured item difficulties. The existing 0–100 scale is retained: Foundation 0–24, Development 25–49, Mastery 50–79, Challenge 80–100. No distribution or quota is imposed, and equal-demand questions can share a score.

The cognitive-demand rubric follows the locally available MathsDatabase `prompts/difficulty-rating-prompt.md`: direct application; routine linked procedures; non-trivial connections or reframing; decisive unsignposted insight. Assess the student-facing task before using its solution to detect hidden demand. Count decisions, representation changes and interpretation, not pages, number of repeated parts, arithmetic bulk or solution length. Account for scaffolding actually present in the question. Rate a multipart task by the demand required to complete it, rather than averaging away its hardest essential part. Fractional numbers alone do not create a higher band. Scores within a band are judgement estimates, not claims of one-point measurement precision.

Evidence includes current question prompts, tables and representation panels, diagram models/descriptions, supplied answers, shared continuation groups and the accepted booklet's teaching sequence (recorded in the Linear Relationships atomisation audit). The assessment retains the table-change/intercept and graphical-solving methods. It does not rewrite questions or answers. The assessed content hash, before/after classification and rationale are recorded in `linear-bank-assessment-register.json`; edited content needs reassessment rather than automatic reuse of its old score.

The library classification describes the main task and other directly assessed skills. Detailed node teaching mappings remain unchanged and can include supporting algebra. The original transfer promoted `equations-from-formulas` to the primary skill on whole pattern pages, including questions with no inverse calculation. Because topic filters use both primary and secondary skills, that made pattern tasks appear under Equations. The correction removes the incidental formula-solving tag from pattern, table and contextual linear-model tasks. Explicit algebra-versus-graph comparison tasks retain it. Given-rule questions use `apply-pattern-equation`, not `pattern-to-equation`.


New theory reviews are blocked from individual question promotion and can be saved inline in teaching modules. New publications require individual assessments rather than ratings inferred from page numbers. Current owner saves follow `booklet-bank-auto-sync.md`; the completed Linear migration tool is retired.
